From download to investor: getting more of Gen Z past the first trade

India adds over 3 million demat accounts a month, but fewer than 1 in 5 accounts trade even once a year. Trackk is built for 20 to 24 year olds who find investing through creators and reels. This is a plan to turn more of those downloads into people who are still investing three months later, without pushing them into options trading, where 89% of traders under 30 lose money.

The tension

Trackk's users are Gen Z (about 90%, average age 20–24), they arrive through creators and colleges, and the app's store listing leads with futures and options. Fast trades look like engagement but burn young users out.

The problem

Most downloads never become investors. When I signed up myself, the PAN step was smooth but bank verification was a dead end: a name mismatch on the ₹1 UPI check, no IFSC option, and no way forward. Others never add money, leave after one IPO, or lose on options and quit.

The plan

Measure "active investor at day 90", find the biggest leak with real data, then fix it with five changes: no dead ends at bank verification, a ₹100 first step, an IPO bridge, a payday plan and an F&O readiness gate.

2. What I found signing up myself (Oct 2026)

I went through Trackk's account opening on my own phone. One person's sign-up proves nothing about size, but it does show where the flow has no way forward.

What is probably going on

  • The ₹1 check is a "reverse penny drop". The user pays ₹1 by UPI, and the app reads the account holder's name, account number and IFSC from that payment, then compares the name with PAN. It's the fastest method and needs no typing, which is why apps use it first.
  • It fails on names that don't match exactly. Joint accounts, initials, swapped first and last names, and middle names all cause this. Many 20–24 year olds have their first account opened with, or by, a parent, so this is likely more common for Trackk's users than for an average broker. That is a hypothesis to size with failure-reason data.
  • The real problem is the missing fallback, not the check. The standard backup is "enter account number + IFSC", then the app sends ₹1 to that account and reads the name back (a "forward penny drop"). That works for almost any bank. Without it, any mismatch or unlisted bank ends the sign-up.
  • The bank list matters less than it looks. Bank of America has no retail banking in India, and Citibank's Indian retail accounts moved to Axis Bank in 2023, so those two are not real gaps. HSBC, Standard Chartered, DBS, and small finance and co-operative banks are, and an IFSC route covers all of them.

The fix, in order of effort

  1. Add the account number + IFSC route with a forward penny drop. Usually a small build, since most verification providers offer both methods; removes the dead end for every bank.
  2. Explain every failure in plain words and say what to do next: "The name on this account is different from your PAN. If it's a joint account, add it with IFSC and upload a bank statement showing your name."
  3. Match names with a score, not exact text. High score: pass. Medium (initials, order, joint holder): pass with a document check by operations. Low: ask for another account.
  4. Joint accounts: allow them where the user is one of the holders, with proof, if compliance confirms this meets SEBI's rule that money must come from the client's own account.
  5. Log a reason code for every failed bank check (name mismatch, bank not supported, UPI failed, user quit) so the size of each problem is known, not guessed.

3. The problem statement

Of every 100 young people who download Trackk, only a handful are still investing three months later. They drop out at bank verification, never add money, leave after one IPO application, or start with options, lose, and quit. Design an onboarding and first-90-days experience that doubles the number of downloads who become active investors, measured by a stricter metric than the industry's, without raising the share of new users whose first trade is in F&O.

4. The metric: active investor at day 90

  • North star: active investor at day 90. In days 61–90 after opening an account, the user holds at least one stock or ETF for delivery (not intraday, not F&O) and either made a new buy or has a running SIP. This is stricter than NSE's "one trade in 12 months" on purpose: it measures a habit, not a visit.
  • Early signal (to decide experiments in 30 days, not 90): by day 30, a second buy in a different week from the first. A second week is the first sign of a habit.
  • Read it two ways: per 100 downloads (judges the whole funnel and acquisition spend) and per 100 accounts opened (judges the product after KYC).
  • Guardrails: share of new users whose first order is F&O (must not rise); F&O losses in the first 90 days for under-25s; notification opt-outs and uninstalls; complaints; brokerage revenue per active user (watched, not optimised).

Why not just "monthly active users"? Opening the app to check a reel-tip stock is not investing. And counting F&O traders as "active" rewards the path most likely to end in a loss and an uninstall.

7. Five changes

1

No dead ends at bank verification

The fix from section 2 above: an account number + IFSC fallback for every bank, plain-language failure messages, score-based name matching, a document route for joint accounts, and a reason code on every failure. Also: save progress, so a user who leaves to find their bank details comes back to the same step, with one reminder. Creator links open on the exact stock the user saw, so curiosity survives sign-up.

2

A ₹100 first step

The first screen after account opening answers "what do I buy with a little money?": three low-priced, diversified ETFs explained in plain words, with the Trackk Score shown beside each. The goal is a first holding the user understands, not a big first trade.

3

"Not allotted?" bridge

When an IPO application isn't allotted and the money is released, show a single choice: keep the money, or put a part into an index ETF or a stock in the same sector as the IPO, with the Trackk Score. IPO-led users stop being one-time visitors.

4

Payday plan

After the first buy, ask one question: "When does money come in?" Offer a small monthly stock or ETF SIP on that date, with a reminder the user set themselves. Habit comes from a date, not from push notifications.

5

F&O readiness gate

F&O unlocks after a short, honest check: at least three weeks with a delivery holding, a five-question risk quiz, and a practice mode with virtual money. This sits on top of SEBI's required risk disclosure. It is not a ban; it is sequencing.

  • Cost: some brokerage in month one.
  • Case for it: under-30 F&O traders lose money 89% of the time, a user who loses in week two rarely returns for SIPs, gold or credit, and the brand promise is "Young India's investment platform", not a trading casino.

9. Experiments

  • Simple user-level A/B tests are fine here. Unlike food delivery or ride-hailing, one user's onboarding doesn't use up a shared resource, so treatment doesn't leak into control. Randomise within each acquisition source (creator, college, organic, paid), because the mix shifts week to week with campaigns.
  • Decide at day 30, confirm at day 90. Use "second buy in a different week by day 30" to make the call; keep the day-90 read running to make sure the early signal didn't lie.
  • Sample size, roughly: to detect a lift from 13% to 15% active investors per account opened (80% power, 5% significance), each arm needs about 4,500 opened accounts. If that's more than a month of Trackk's sign-ups, test the biggest change first, or use the day-30 signal, which moves more.
  • A dead end is a bug, not an experiment. Ship the IFSC fallback and clear error messages to everyone and measure before vs after. Only the name-match threshold gets an A/B test, because loosening it trades sign-ups against fraud risk and needs a number on both.
  • Test order after that: whichever leak the real data shows is biggest. With the assumed numbers, the ₹100 first step, then the payday plan, then the gate and the IPO bridge.
  • The gate gets its own test with brokerage revenue per user as a tracked cost, so the trade-off is a number the team decides on, not a debate.

10. SQL

PostgreSQL. Table names are assumed: users, kyc_events, accounts, fund_transfers, orders, holdings_daily, sips, ipo_applications.

1. Download-to-day-90 funnel by acquisition source 
WITH base AS (
  SELECT u.user_id, u.acq_source, date_trunc('week', u.installed_at) AS cohort_week,
         a.opened_at,
         (SELECT min(f.created_at) FROM fund_transfers f WHERE f.user_id = u.user_id AND f.status = 'SUCCESS') AS first_fund_at,
         (SELECT min(o.created_at) FROM orders o WHERE o.user_id = u.user_id AND o.status = 'EXECUTED') AS first_order_at
  FROM users u LEFT JOIN accounts a ON a.user_id = u.user_id
),
active90 AS (   -- active investor in days 61-90 after account opening
  SELECT b.user_id
  FROM base b
  WHERE b.opened_at IS NOT NULL
    AND EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM holdings_daily h
                WHERE h.user_id = b.user_id AND h.product IN ('DELIVERY_EQ','ETF')
                  AND h.as_of = (b.opened_at + interval '90 days')::date AND h.qty > 0)
    AND (EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM orders o WHERE o.user_id = b.user_id AND o.side = 'BUY'
                   AND o.product IN ('DELIVERY_EQ','ETF') AND o.status = 'EXECUTED'
                   AND o.created_at BETWEEN b.opened_at + interval '61 days' AND b.opened_at + interval '90 days')
      OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM sips s WHERE s.user_id = b.user_id AND s.status = 'ACTIVE'
                   AND s.created_at <= b.opened_at + interval '90 days'))
)
SELECT acq_source, cohort_week,
       count(*)                                   AS downloads,
       count(opened_at)                           AS accounts,
       count(first_fund_at)                       AS funded,
       count(first_order_at)                      AS first_order,
       count(a90.user_id)                         AS active_investor_d90,
       round(100.0 * count(a90.user_id) / count(*), 1)                 AS per_100_downloads,
       round(100.0 * count(a90.user_id) / nullif(count(opened_at),0), 1) AS per_100_accounts
FROM base b LEFT JOIN active90 a90 USING (user_id)
WHERE cohort_week <= now() - interval '100 days'   -- only cohorts old enough to have a day-90 read
GROUP BY 1, 2 ORDER BY 2, 1;
2. Where KYC stalls: last step reached, and how many come back 
WITH last_step AS (
  SELECT user_id,
         (array_agg(step ORDER BY created_at DESC))[1] AS last_step,   -- e.g. PAN, AADHAAR, BANK, SELFIE, ESIGN, DONE
         count(DISTINCT created_at::date)              AS days_with_activity
  FROM kyc_events GROUP BY user_id
)
SELECT last_step,
       count(*)                                                   AS users,
       round(100.0 * count(*) / sum(count(*)) OVER (), 1)         AS pct_of_starters,
       round(100.0 * avg((days_with_activity > 1)::int), 1)       AS pct_who_returned_another_day
FROM last_step GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY users DESC;
2b. Bank verification: how often each method fails, why, and who recovers 
SELECT bv.method,                                  -- 'UPI_REVERSE_PENNY', 'MANUAL_BANK_LIST', 'IFSC_FORWARD_PENNY'
       bv.failure_reason,                          -- NULL = passed; 'NAME_MISMATCH', 'BANK_NOT_SUPPORTED', 'UPI_FAILED', 'USER_EXIT'
       bv.bank_name,
       count(*)                                                             AS attempts,
       count(DISTINCT bv.user_id)                                           AS users,
       round(100.0 * avg(bv.name_match_score), 0)                           AS avg_name_score,
       round(100.0 * count(DISTINCT a.user_id) / count(DISTINCT bv.user_id), 1) AS pct_who_still_opened_account
FROM bank_verifications bv
LEFT JOIN accounts a ON a.user_id = bv.user_id
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3
ORDER BY users DESC
LIMIT 50;
-- If NAME_MISMATCH and BANK_NOT_SUPPORTED users rarely open an account, the fallback is missing, not the user's will.
3. Opened but never funded: how long people wait, and who never does 
SELECT a.acq_source_bucket,
       count(*)                                                                     AS accounts,
       percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY extract(epoch FROM f.first_fund - a.opened_at)/3600) AS median_hours_to_fund,
       round(100.0 * avg((f.first_fund IS NULL OR f.first_fund > a.opened_at + interval '14 days')::int), 1) AS pct_not_funded_by_day_14
FROM (SELECT a.*, u.acq_source AS acq_source_bucket FROM accounts a JOIN users u USING (user_id)) a
LEFT JOIN LATERAL (SELECT min(created_at) AS first_fund FROM fund_transfers
                   WHERE user_id = a.user_id AND status = 'SUCCESS') f ON true
WHERE a.opened_at <= now() - interval '15 days'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY accounts DESC;
4. Day-90 outcome by first path: investing-first, IPO-only, F&O-first 
WITH first_path AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT ON (o.user_id) o.user_id,
         CASE WHEN o.product IN ('FUT','OPT') THEN 'FNO_FIRST'
              WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM ipo_applications i WHERE i.user_id = o.user_id
                           AND i.applied_at <= o.created_at)
                   AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM ipo_applications i WHERE i.user_id = o.user_id AND i.allotted)
                   THEN 'IPO_NOT_ALLOTTED'
              ELSE 'INVESTING_FIRST' END AS path
  FROM orders o WHERE o.status = 'EXECUTED'
  ORDER BY o.user_id, o.created_at
)
SELECT path, count(*) AS users,
       round(100.0 * avg((a90.user_id IS NOT NULL)::int), 1) AS pct_active_investor_d90
FROM first_path fp LEFT JOIN active90 a90 USING (user_id)     -- active90 as defined in query 1
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY users DESC;
5. Guardrail: young users whose first 30 days included F&O, and their losses 
SELECT exp.variant,
       count(DISTINCT u.user_id)                                                          AS new_accounts_under_25,
       round(100.0 * count(DISTINCT o.user_id) / count(DISTINCT u.user_id), 1)          AS pct_fno_in_first_30_days,
       round(avg(pnl.realised_fno_pnl) FILTER (WHERE o.user_id IS NOT NULL), 0)          AS avg_fno_pnl_first_90_days
FROM users u
JOIN accounts a  ON a.user_id = u.user_id
JOIN experiment_assignments exp ON exp.user_id = u.user_id AND exp.experiment = 'fno_readiness_gate'
LEFT JOIN orders o ON o.user_id = u.user_id AND o.product IN ('FUT','OPT') AND o.status = 'EXECUTED'
                  AND o.created_at < a.opened_at + interval '30 days'
LEFT JOIN LATERAL (SELECT sum(realised_pnl) AS realised_fno_pnl FROM pnl_daily p
                   WHERE p.user_id = u.user_id AND p.segment = 'FNO'
                     AND p.as_of < a.opened_at + interval '90 days') pnl ON true
WHERE date_part('year', age(a.opened_at, u.date_of_birth)) < 25
GROUP BY 1;
-- Treatment must show a lower pct_fno_in_first_30_days. If it doesn't, the gate isn't working.

12. Risks and what could prove this wrong

  • The funnel numbers are mine, not Trackk's, and the sign-up finding is one person. If bank failures turn out to be rare, the work starts elsewhere. The calculator exists so the order of work follows the data.
  • The gate costs revenue. For a young broker, F&O brokerage may be most of revenue. The test makes the cost visible; the founders decide.
  • Looser name matching can let in the wrong account. Money must come only from the client's own bank account. Medium-score matches go through a document check, and fraud and payout-reversal rates are guardrails on that test.
  • Nudges can annoy. Every reminder is one the user chose (payday date, KYC step). Watch opt-outs and uninstalls as guardrails.
  • Suggestions must not become advice. ETF and stock suggestions are educational and broad-based, never personalised "buy this" calls. Compliance reviews every screen.
  • Day 90 is slow. The day-30 signal could mislead. Keep the day-90 read on every test.

Why I built this

I'm 22, which puts me inside Trackk's user base, and most of my friends' first investments followed the exact paths above: a reel, an IPO, an options tip. I signed up for Trackk myself and got stuck at bank verification, which is why it leads this plan. At EnKash I owned merchant onboarding for the acquiring product, so I have seen how one missing document stalls a sign-up. For CoinDCX I mapped 12 events from sign-up to first trade and designed an A/B test for the biggest KYC drop-off. A year in fraud and AML at Stripe taught me that the guardrail is part of the product, not an afterthought. Trackk's next stage is turning downloads into investors who stay; that's the problem I want to work on.
